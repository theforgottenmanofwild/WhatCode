# Appearance

Appearance changes how the app and the editor look: the theme, the accent color, the editor font, the spacing and the colors of the code. Every choice is saved at once and used on every screen.

## Opening it

Open **⋮ > Settings** and tap **Appearance** under **Look**. A picture of the editor at the top shows each choice as you make it.

## Light, Dark or System

- **Light** uses bright surfaces with strong contrast.
- **Dark** uses low-glare colors for dim rooms.
- **System** follows your device's light or dark setting, and the app changes with it. This is the default.

**True black** under **Dark style** swaps the dark grey backgrounds of the dark theme for black. It saves battery on OLED screens and has no effect in the light theme.

## Accent color

Pick **Blue**, **Teal**, **Green**, **Orange**, **Purple** or **Pink**. The accent is used for buttons, switches, links, the cursor, the selection and the status bar. Every accent has a light and a dark shade, so text on it stays easy to read in both themes. Blue is the default.

## Editor text

- **Editor font** is **Monospace**, **Serif monospace** or **Sans-serif**. They all come with Android. Sans-serif is easier on the eye, but the columns of a file do not line up in it.
- **Font size** goes from 10 to 28 sp. The default is 15.
- **Line height** goes from 100% to 200% in steps of 10. The default is 100%.
- **Margins** are **Compact**, **Normal** or **Comfortable**, and set how much room there is round the text.
- **Highlight the current line** gives the line with the cursor a soft background. It is off by default.
- **Syntax highlighting** under **Syntax colors** colors keywords, strings, comments and the rest by the language of the file. It is on by default. With it off the text keeps one color; your syntax colors are kept for when you turn it on again.

## Syntax colors

Tap **Syntax colors** to choose your own color for keywords, control words, types, functions, constants, properties, strings, numbers, comments, regular expressions and CSS selectors. Choose **Light** or **Dark** at the top first: the two themes keep their own colors.

Tap a row to open its color editor. Move the **Red**, **Green** and **Blue** sliders, or type a code such as `#C586C0`, and the sample shows the result on the real editor background. Tap **Use this color** to keep it, **Default** to go back to the original color, or **Cancel**.

A color that would be hard to read on the editor background is shown in the nearest shade that is easy to read, and the editor tells you which one. This also keeps your colors readable if you turn on true black later.

## Return to default

**Return to default** at the bottom of the page puts everything back: System mode, Blue, normal black, the default font, size and spacing, syntax highlighting on, and every syntax color. It asks you to confirm first. The same button on the **Syntax colors** page only resets the colors. Your files and your other settings are not touched.
